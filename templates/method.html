How Overlap works

Two parts: what it works out for every route, and how it decides which changes to recommend. The terms used in your results are explained below.

{{ how_it_works() }}

Terms used in your results

CO₂e
Carbon dioxide equivalent. Every greenhouse gas is counted as the amount of CO₂ with the same warming effect, so emissions can be added up.
Route
All the orders that travel between the same two places by the same transport mode, added up over the file.
Estimated annual saving
What a route costs or emits today, minus what it would on the proposed mode, treating the file as one year of orders. A planning estimate, not a quote.
Re-runs, or stress test
Each route change is worked out again {{ confidence_checks }} times with the cost and emission rates varied up to {{ pct(factor_spread) }} either way, to see whether it still cuts both.
Confidence
High: the change held up in at least {{ pct(confidence_high) }} of re-runs. Moderate: at least {{ pct(confidence_moderate) }}. Low: fewer. Not stress-tested: a change that is not a transport mode switch, which says what its figure depends on.
Recommended now, worth reviewing, needs more data
How ready a change is to act on. Recommended now means high confidence. Worth reviewing means moderate confidence, or worked out from your data without a stress test. Needs more data means low confidence, or a figure that depends on something your file does not contain.
