How it works

Three steps, from a spreadsheet to a decision

  1. You give the tool

    A CSV file of your company's orders

    These five columns are required, and everything else is optional.

    • origin_name
    • origin_city
    • dest_city
    • weight_kg
    • mode

    Example row

    Antigua Microlots, Antigua, Bristol, 120.0, air

  2. The tool works out

    What each route really costs you

    Orders going the same way, by the same transport mode, are added together into one route. For that route, the tool works out two numbers side by side: what it costs in dollars, and how much carbon it produces, based on the same facts each time, how much was shipped, how far, and how it travelled.

    The tool then checks what would happen if that route used a different mode instead, for example switching an air route to sea, rail or road.

  3. You get back

    Only the changes actually worth making

    A route is only flagged if switching its mode would cut both its cost and its carbon by at least a quarter. A change that saves money but adds carbon does not count, and neither does the reverse.

How this is different from what already exists

Carbon reporting tools

They tell you what a route emits but never what it costs, so they cannot tell you which one is worth fixing first.

Freight spend analytics

They tell you what a route costs, using your real invoices rather than an estimate, but they ignore carbon entirely.

Network design software

Does both properly with an optimisation solver, but needs a six month project and a modelling team to get there.

This

Prices every route on cost and carbon together and ranks what to change, working from a spreadsheet export in about a second.

Why you can check it

  • Published factors Emissions per tonne-km come from the DEFRA and GLEC ranges, the basis of most freight carbon reporting.
  • Error bars, not a single number Those factors are published as ranges rather than fixed values, so the whole ranking is re-run 2,000 times and the saving is quoted as a range.
  • No model, no training data It is arithmetic on your own figures. Every formula is written out, with its source and what it does not account for.
  • Your file never lands on a disk It is read in memory and dropped as soon as the analysis is done. What happens to your file.
