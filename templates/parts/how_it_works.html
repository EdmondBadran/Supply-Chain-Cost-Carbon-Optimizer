1 · What it works out

A yearly cost and carbon figure for every route

You upload a year of shipping orders. Orders that travel between the same two places by the same transport mode are grouped into one route, so thousands of order lines become a few dozen routes.

For each route, the weight shipped times the distance times a standard rate for its mode gives a yearly freight cost and CO2e. Warehouse, packaging and returns are added on top. Carbon rates follow DEFRA and GLEC, the basis of most freight carbon reporting. Cost rates are typical industry rates, not your contract prices.

2 · What it recommends

Only changes that cut cost and carbon together

Each route is priced again on every realistic alternative, such as air to sea on a long route or road to rail. A change is recommended only if it cuts both the route's cost and its carbon by at least {{ pct(flag_threshold) }}. The list is ranked by what each change saves.

Every rate is then varied and the check run again {{ confidence_checks }} times. High confidence means a change still paid off in at least {{ pct(confidence_high) }} of those runs. The figures are screening estimates for deciding where to look, not quotes.
