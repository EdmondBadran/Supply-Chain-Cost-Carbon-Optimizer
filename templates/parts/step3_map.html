What if the network were shaped differently

Close a site and its work moves to whichever remaining site is nearest and has room, and the building stops costing you. Open one and the routes closer to it move across. Every route is reassigned and repriced, not estimated.

A site with a stated capacity is held to it. Without one it is assumed to absorb up to half as much again as it handles today, and every site says which of the two it used. Past that the overflow goes to the next nearest, and the answer says so. A new site is priced at what your own sites cost and burn per tonne, on an average grid, because nothing in your data describes a building that does not exist yet.
